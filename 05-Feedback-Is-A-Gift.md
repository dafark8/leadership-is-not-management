# Chapter 5 Feedback is a Gift

Feedback should be timely. Don’t wait for reviews to share praise and always provide critical feedback and growth opportunities in a timely manner. 

One consideration with feedback is how does your team member like to receive feedback? Some of this is asked in the Today and Beyond document and Team Norms. But what we say, and what we mean, may be different. 

Some team members like feedback immediately during a call. They are less bothered by an “audience” than not knowing something immediately. However the audience may be uncomfortable and I suggest against this. 

More often, team members want to know their growth opportunities privately. There is a lot of nuance here. Some are open to an unexpected message to chat right after a meeting. Others want to decompress and reflect before discussing their growth opportunities. Email, messenger, or phone call, every person is different and it is important as a Leader to adapt to your team members’ needs, not your own preferred communication style. 

Regardless of how your team accepts feedback, it is beneficial to follow up in writing. This could be a 1-2 sentence summary or something longer depending on the type of feedback. Include successes as well as growth opportunities and be sure to include clear, actionable next steps. 

The above focuses on growth opportunities. That’s because we should praise publicly and critique privately. It is a Leader’s responsibility to elevate and promote our teams’ successes broadly (as well as privately) and to coach individuals in safer environments. 

## Approaching Feedback

It is easy when offering feedback to be defensive, or posturing the “right” way something could have been done. Remember everyone wants to do their best work. Expect the best intentions and seek a perspective to understand motivations. 

Most importantly, remember **clear is kind**. Individuals don't make progress through abstract feedback or guessing. Be clear. Be kind. Be actionable. 

| Don’t Say | Instead Try |
| --- | --- | 
| This was done poorly | What were your goals with this? | 
| The problem with the meeting was | How do you feel today’s meeting went? | 
| This is not good | How do you feel the stakeholder responded to this? |
| This solution (e.g., design) is not good | This design feels busy, what is something we could take away and still deliver? | 
| I told you to improve your skill set. You are still not showing growth. | I know we are working on X skillset. Help me understand how today’s design/meeting/artifact addresses those goals? | 


In each of these alternatives, we come to the conversation from a position of inquiry, kindness, and allowing the team member to drive the conversation. This list is by no means inclusive but hopefully demonstrates a positive spin on growth and coaching opportunities. 

## Receiving Feedback
It’s important as a Leader, to be open to feedback yourself. Feedback may come in many forms and the key is to listen and to adjust. Good feedback can come from anyone. 

I’ve received feedback from my team in the moment “we have too many meetings” or in team retrospectives “we are confused when you do X”. 

I’ve received feedback from my manager, about praise from my team, or concerns they may have shared. This has included "David you are too academic" and also "David you need to explain this more before" - so it has been a balance of lessons. 

I’ve received feedback from my peers and how I, or my team collaborate with theirs. This has included escalation paths and general ways of working. 

In some cases, I ask for this feedback “how am I doing” and in other cases someone may say “David, someone came to me and expressed feedback (both positive and growth-oriented)”. Regardless of the reason and source, I try to keep an open mind. I try to listen to the feedback, taking it from a place of care. I try not to defend or rationalize. Certainly not in the moment. 

**Feedback is a gift.** I accept that gift, and then decide how to proceed. I don’t respond at the moment. If a day, or a week later I have questions and seek clarification, I may invite further conversations and understanding.
