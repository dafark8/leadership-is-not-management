# Chapter 6 Managing Meetings
Take all the team meetings, stakeholder meetings, 1:1s meetings, and feedback sessions. Regardless of who we are meeting with and how, there is one common noun in all of these blocks of time. They are meetings. 

How do we run good meetings? Throughout my career I’ve had the opportunity to collaborate on a number of meeting hygiene teams. Whether a critique, a one-on-one, a brainstorming session, or product roadmap, below are some of the best practices I seek to include in my meetings. 

## Meeting Roles
There are three key roles to any meeting. Depending on the meeting size some roles may be held by the same person. For instance a 1:1 may have the Organizer and Notetaker the same person (Leader) and the Accountability owner the team member. 

| | Overview | Key Responsibilities | 
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meeting Owner | The Meeting Owner schedules the meeting, ensures an agenda, and shares the recording when necessary | <ul><li>Schedule Invitation</li><li>Include Agenda</li><li>List Required individuals in agenda</li></ul>|
| Nimble Notetaker | Captures action items during meeting and publishes via the predetermined team space | <ul><li>Take summary-level notes</li><li>Add the Recording Link to the notes</li></ul>| 
|Accountability Ace | Acts as TimeCop during meetings, supporting the Meeting Owner in facilitation and keeping to the agenda. Follows up with Nimble Notetaker to ensure notes are published and follows up with the team to ensure items are completed. | <ul><li>Ensure meeting is recorded </li><li>Coordinate with Meeting Owner the agenda and timeline</li><li>Follow up with individuals on action items</li><li>Coordinate with team tracking action items in shared tool (e.g., Jira)</li></ul> | 

###  Some Details
**Listing Required Individuals.** Yes, Outlook and other tools allow you to mark folks as Optional which is helpful for scheduling, but in my experience nobody reads that. Be explicit in the invite who is required and who is optional. Simply saying “required: David, Jamie, Ben; Optional: All Others” is sufficient. 

**Taking Summary-level notes.** Nobody needs a transcript. What are the topics discussed and what are the action items? Be brief. Don’t rely on folks watching recordings. 

*A note on Artificial Intelligence* AI is part of our workflow. If you are using AI in meetings, ensure: 
1. You are following organizational guidelines 
2. You state / are clear AI is being used, and how 
3. You ensure a human reviews all outputs for accuracy and completion. AI hallucinates. We want to be mindful of slop and where AI supports, or may lead us astray. 

**Coordinate and Track Action Items.** We are all busy, and often running from meeting to meeting. Be mindful of overscheduling and build in time to check in on action items. 

## Meeting Norms
Roles are only one part of hosting and facilitating good meetings. I encourage you to read Kevin Hoffman’s [Meeting Design](https://rosenfeldmedia.com/books/meeting-design/) for comprehensive tips on a variety of meeting facilitation. Some of my highlights for meetings are noted below. 

### Before the Meeting
| Positive Behavior | Avoid | Example | 
| --- | ---| --- | 
| Schedule meetings at :05 and 0:35 | Scheduling meetings at the top of the hour to “end” five minutes early |  1:05 - 1:30 Weekly 1:1 | 
| Clear Invitation Names | Blank Meeting Titles | Project Name Sprint 2 Review of User Story (#NNN) | 
| Project Names or Epics | Generic Meeting Titles (e.g., Sync) | Project Name Sprint 2 Review of User Story (#NNN) | 
| Add an agenda and update before meeting | No Agenda | Include links to documents and anticipated time blocks
|List Required and Optional Attendees | Create a long list of attendees | Required: David, Amy, Alex; Optional: All others |
| Schedule with ample prep |Same day or within 8-business hours | Scheduled 1+ business day in advance |
| Confirm the need for the meeting | Don’t hold a meeting just because it’s on the calendar | If something is addressed asynchronously (e.g., a sprint is postponed, cancel the meeting) |


### During the Meeting
| Positive Behavior | Avoid | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Be specific on the meeting type / purpose | Jumping directly into a deck without context | “Thank you for joining today’s critique/roadmap review /etc” | 
| End Early | Disrespecting other’s time | “I’ll give you back some time”. Three minutes is not a gift to do work. It’s a humane bio break. | 
| Have a TimeCop (Accountability Ace) | Running over time | “Today I’m going to ask Jamie to help keep us on schedule” | 
| Watch for participation | Allow the loudest (or highest paid) person to hog air time | Create an inclusive space for feedback allowing for multiple channels (voice, text, etc) of contribution |  
| Provide Relevant Links in Advance | Don’t assume attendees have pre-read information |  Allow time in the agenda to review provided documents |  
| Be Present | Showing up and multitasking | Lead by example and close other windows | 
| Camera on when speaking | Being a disembodied voice | You may keep your camera off but when you plan to speak, turn the camera on | 
| Record if necessary and ask for permission | Record out of habit | Often we don’t watch recordings. What happens to them? Encourage actionable and meaningful notes. | 
| Honor the agenda | Derailing the agenda | Ask for a sandbox / parking lot to put other items and coordinate separately | 


### After the Meeting
| Positive Behavior | Avoid | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Send meeting minutes | Storing minutes locally | Store meeting minutes on a team shared drive | 
| Send action items | Assume minutes include all action items | Here is the <link/> to the meeting minutes and the key action items discussed: <ul><li>Bob to do X</li><li>Amy to do Y</li><li> Max to do Z</li></ul> |
| Schedule Follow ups | Delay in scheduling the next meeting. Keep that momentum | In the Action Items “our next sync is on mm/dd to review Action Items X and Y” | 


These are by no means an all inclusive list, nor do they work in all settings and situations. These are my litmus tests, and my starting point with teams and organizations.
