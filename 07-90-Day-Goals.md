# Chapter 7 Your (First) 90 Day Goals

The last few sections have looked at team meetings, team norms, and building rapport across the organization. Ultimately we are all measured on what we deliver, and our value to the business. Let’s talk about your first 90-days in a new role. Rather than focus on quantifiable goals for your first 3-months in a role, here are a few qualitative milestones I outline for myself in any new Leadership position: 

## Month 1 Goals 

### Week 1 Goals
Week one, apart from getting connected, is really about meeting with my team and those in my Leadership circle. The goal isn’t about work, but simply building rapport and familiarity. 

### Week 2 Goals

Where week 1 is general introductions, week 2 starts looking at the Today and Beyond conversations. I’ll start scheduling regular 1:1s with my team and start meeting cross-functional leads, scheduling recurring meetings as appropriate. Note, I say I am scheduling these meetings. I do not expect to complete all these meetings in my second-week on the job. 

### Weeks 3 and 4

By the end of month 1, I have my recurring 1:1s with my team, my manager, and my peer group established on the calendar. I should also have: 
- Formed or refined a Team Charter 
- Met with business stakeholders my team engages with 
- Established weekly 1:1s with my team 
- Established weekly or biweekly 1:1s with my manager 
- Established recurring 1:1s with peer leaders and stakeholders

## Month 2-3 Goals 

Month two and three are about turning some of these new rituals into routine. Focus on building trust. As I mentioned about Random 1:1s in [Chapter 4](04-Recurring-Meetings.md), I use month 2 to start meeting with the expanded network of friendly names and faces across the organization. 

As time is precious for everyone, offer an agenda in your invitation. Something along the lines of *“Hi, I am a new X in Y role. So and so suggested we chat. If this time is not good, let me know. I want to introduce ourselves and get to know you and your needs. Thanks”*

From a work-perspective, I start prioritizing, or documenting (read: externalizing knowledge) about existing work streams and perceived priorities. I will want to start hypothesizing approaches to streamline work and introduce or adjust different practices across the team. 

## Month 4-6 Goals 

One theme should be clear in the first three months: there are very few quantifiable deliverables. This time is about building relationships, building a mental model (and externalizing that model) of how the business works. You are settling in, expressing a curious mindset and laying the foundation for the work ahead. 

Looking beyond those first 90-days, how might you prioritize change? Evaluate the organization’s, your team’s, and your own roadmaps. Identify quick wins you can deliver with your own role and your team. This is a time to evaluate quickly and often - through retrospectives, critiques, or other mechanisms for change. Months’ 4-6 often focus on a single MVP demonstrating you and your team’s value with the goal to scale and iterate in an evolutionary manner. 

## Your Mileage May Vary

I intentionally avoided concrete goals in my examples above. Nothing like “ship 5 features” or “expand team through training”. Every organization and team is different. Some of these goals may be illustrated in your job description. Hopefully your goals are communicated  via OKRs, KPIs, SMART goals or something else. I am not here to tell you what your tangible goals are. I’m not here to tell you anything really. But this content is intended to illustrate some of the softer skills and goals I strive for as a new Leader on your team.
