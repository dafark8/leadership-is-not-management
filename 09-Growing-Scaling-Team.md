# Chapter 9 - Growing (Scaling) Your Team

As a leader or a manager, we all hope our teams grow. I have a few standard operating procedures around interviewing and hiring. As with everything, these are not one size fits all. I adjust based on the team’s and organization’s needs and practices. 

## Standard Interview Process  

One frustration of job seekers is an unclear, or inconsistent, interview process. Not knowing how many interviews, the order, or what the substance of an interview is (behavioral conversation or case study) can be jarring to candidates and interviewers alike. I once asked a recruiter the format of an interview and was told it would be a behavioral interview, only to arrive and the panel expected a case-study presentation. Needless to say I did not get the job with that company and the overall feeling left me disoriented. 

There are a lot of opinions on the appropriate number of interviews. While I commend folks who can hire after one or two interviews, I think we need more than that. 7+ is far too many. I target 3-4 conversations before a decision. My typical structure for an interview cadence is as follows: 

**1. Recruiter PreScreen.** After a candidate’s resume is green lit by the hiring manager and talent team, the first screening is a 15-30 minute phone call between the recruiter and the candidate. This is an opportunity to verify basic credentials, interest in the role, alignment with salary expectations, and to answer (on both sides of the dialogue) any key questions. 

**2. Hiring Manager Behavioral Interview.** Passing the PreScreen, the hiring manager has a 30-45 minute call with the candidate where they can ask a series of behavioral questions. It is key to reserve at least 10-minutes for the candidate to ask questions of the hiring manager. Now I've been on teams that forgo the HM screen and go straight to the team panel(s). Personally I find the HM screen helps weed out a good portion of folks and one half hour of my time is much less than cumulative hours of group phone calls. 

**3. Team Panel(s).** Ideally candidates are subjected to a single, 1-hour panel. I realize different organizations have different structures. I've see production level roles have one round. Senior roles having 2 or more. And some organizations have prescribed technical, behavioral, and portfolio reviews. Regardless of how many, the framework for conversations should typically follow: introductions (5 minutes), case-studies or experience-driven questions (35 minutes), panel Q&A (10 minutes), candidate questions (10 minutes). 

**4. Offer and Reverse Interview!** These two events are likely not at the same time. Assuming an offer is extended, I find benefit to myself as a Leader and as a candidate to schedule one additional call with the hiring manager to ask clarifying questions. Some organizations call this a matching call. Often there is more candor after an offer is extended as there is greater trust. 

## Standard Interview Questions 

A series of 4 interviews is great but interview cadence is only half the battle. We need consistency in our interview questions so every candidate is evaluated on the same rubric. 

I divide interview questions by generic questions and then by level and role. I keep questions open-ended so that I don’t have to ask every question to get a read of a candidate, and I seek to ask enough to merit an informed decision based on consistent data. 

It is important to have a standard rubric for evaluating responses. This way candidates are graded on consistently, even if the interview panel changes across candidates. Rubrics are not prescriptive but rather descriptive. For instance when asking “tell me about a conflict and how you sought resolution” I am less interested in the specific steps and more interested in how you communicate, share responsibility, and achieve a workable resolution. 

I’ve gone back and forth on “do i share my interview questions and rubric here” and I’ve settled on not sharing these questions. Not because I believe they are proprietary but rather because to share the full list would be exhausting and it would put pretense on preparation when I value interviewing your natural self. I want you to prepare, and in some cases I may share a subset of questions in advance, but this is not the environment for that. 


## Take Home Tests
I am against take-home tests. They burden individuals who have responsibilities outside of work (parents, caregivers, etc), and they often smell of “free work”. 

That said, if your organization does insist on take home tests, a few guidelines: 
1. They should be unrelated to your business. There should be ZERO perception that this is work for free. The one take home test I ever completed was to design the 1,000 floor elevator. I declined others that asked me to review the company’s existing service. 
2. They should be realistically timeboxed. 
3. They should be compensated. You don’t need to pay a full consulting rate for a take home test, but if you time box the exercise to “2-4 hours” expect to pay $150/candidate (thinking a $35-75/hour rate for 2-4 hours) or something similar based in reality. 

## Making Decisions on Candidates 

Some teams require unanimous agreement, others mixed. I won't speak strongly if there is a right or wrong approach, I believe it varies by org. Find what works for you. One colleague asks their panelists “who will go to bat for this candidate” which I find a great way to take ownership of choice and build agency within your team. 

## Shared Chat Space
After a candidate makes it past the hiring manager interview (Step 2 above), I work with the Talent team to spin up a private chat space including myself, the recruiter, and members of the panel. I typically name these something akin to “Interview Panel | Role | Name”. Again, follow organizational norms and best practices. 

This allows for streamlined communication around scheduling, sharing of notes and questions, and provides a clear back channel for the team during the interview process. After the interview, I ask panelists to share their feedback structured around Pros, Cons, Risks/Concerns and their individual leanings (hire/continue or not). 

After the interviews are complete and a decision is made, I remove all members from the chat except for myself and the talent team.
